## Why this pool is suspended

Three candidate static-visual approaches were explored:

1. understandable mechanism / partial access;
2. incomplete visual pattern;
3. visual anomaly / unexpected relation.

Independent red-team reviews converged on the same methodological problem:

- mechanism / partial access strongly collapses into CS, concealment curiosity, technical familiarity, or completion bias;
- visual pattern strongly collapses into CS, puzzle-solving, ability, and completion bias;
- visual anomaly strongly collapses into novelty, salience, error detection, aesthetics, and CS ambiguity.

The core question was:

> Can a static scene create a meaningful choice between investing attention to understand more and not investing more attention, without simply becoming a CS or CR stimulus?

Current answer: **not demonstrated**.

## What this exploration taught us

The useful lesson is not that epistemic curiosity is unimportant.

The lesson is that **epistemic curiosity is not cleanly operationalized as a peer AW stimulus axis through these static-image manipulations**.

A participant may express further-investment language inside a CS or CR reaction context, but that evidence should be evaluated as part of a possible **domain-specific response trajectory**, not used to justify an AW-targeting image pair.
