## Purpose

This document explores three narrow static-visual candidates for the epistemic reaction-context family under the redefined AW layer.

The design question is deliberately small:

> Can a static scene create a meaningful choice between investing attention to understand more and not investing more attention, without simply becoming a CS or CR stimulus?

None of the concepts below is an accepted stimulus. None carries AW polarity.

Each uses:

```text
target_signal_hypothesis: AW
reaction_context_family: epistemic
hypothesized_direction: unconfirmed
signal_mapping_status: NONE
```

The candidates are evaluated with the working triad:

> **CONTROL + EXPERIENCE + INTERPRETABILITY**

---

# Candidate E1 — Understandable mechanism

## Scene

A visually attractive, non-branded mechanical object or kinetic assembly with several visible parts and one locally unusual but non-threatening relationship between components. The object should look complete enough to be plausible but open enough that a viewer could want to inspect how it works.

Examples of surface realization may include a compact mechanical linkage, modular object, or unfamiliar but coherent everyday mechanism. Avoid recognizable consumer brands, weapons, damage, or obvious failure.

## Manipulated property candidate

Visibility / accessibility of the functional relationship between two components while keeping the object, scene, camera, light and overall information density controlled.

## Experience tension

One realization may invite closer inspection because the mechanism relationship is easier to trace.

The other may remain visually coherent but require more inferential investment to understand.

Both must be worth looking at; neither may look broken or defective.

## Plausible reaction space

- `Noriu suprasti, kaip tai veikia.`
- `Įdomu, kaip tos dalys susijusios.`
- `Man užtenka to, ką matau.`
- `Per daug techninių detalių — neįdomu.`
- `Šitas tiesiog gražesnis.`
- `Sunku įvardyti.`

## Main confounds

- **CS contamination:** wanting to understand the mechanism may simply reflect wanting more clarity.
- technical familiarity / engineering knowledge
- aesthetics of mechanical complexity
- competence / mastery associations
- novelty

## Falsification question

If most relevant reactions are naturally expressed as `noriu daugiau aiškumo` rather than `noriu investuoti dėmesį / suprasti`, E1 may belong primarily to CS rather than AW.

## Status

**PROMISING / HIGH CS-CONFOUND RISK**

# Candidate E2 — Incomplete visual pattern

## Scene

A visually elegant arrangement containing a coherent but non-trivial pattern. The pattern is perceivable without text, numbers, mathematical notation, cultural symbols, or a task instruction.

Possible surface realizations: abstract tiles, lights, stones, geometric objects, repeated forms, or spatial sequences.

## Manipulated property candidate

The amount or placement of pattern evidence needed to make the underlying regularity easier or harder to infer, while preserving overall visual quality and element count where possible.

## Experience tension

One realization may create a stronger pull to search for the rule or relationship.

The other may feel sufficiently complete or less worth further attention.

The scene must not become a puzzle with a single correct answer; ConflictLab is not testing puzzle-solving ability.

## Plausible reaction space

- `Noriu suprasti, koks čia dėsningumas.`
- `Užkabino, nes kažkas nesutampa.`
- `Man neįdomu ieškoti taisyklės.`
- `Pakanka tiesiog pažiūrėti.`
- `Patinka ritmas / kompozicija.`
- `Sunku įvardyti.`

## Main confounds

- problem-solving ability
- need for closure
- CS ambiguity
- aesthetic symmetry
- puzzle framing / correct-answer expectation

## Falsification question

If the choice becomes mainly `can solve vs cannot solve`, `clear vs unclear`, or `symmetrical vs asymmetrical`, the candidate fails as epistemic-AW and should not be rescued by cue wording.

## Status

**HIGH-RISK / WORTH CONCEPT TESTING**

---

# Candidate E3 — Visual anomaly / unexpected relation

## Scene

A calm, attractive everyday or architectural scene containing one subtle, non-threatening anomaly: an element whose relationship to the scene is unusual enough to invite inspection but not so bizarre that it becomes a gimmick.

The anomaly must be physically plausible and emotionally neutral.

Examples might include an unexpected but possible object relationship, repeated element with one controlled deviation, or unfamiliar spatial relation.

## Manipulated property candidate

Presence / degree of a single controlled anomaly while preserving scene composition, salience, object identity and aesthetic quality as much as possible.

## Experience tension

One realization may invite the viewer to look again because something appears worth understanding.

The alternative may remain coherent and aesthetically satisfying without creating the same inspection pull.

Equal legitimacy is critical: the anomaly cannot simply be an error that every participant wants corrected.

## Plausible reaction space

- `Kažkas čia užkabino — noriu suprasti kodėl.`
- `Norisi pažiūrėti dar kartą.`
- `Man tai tiesiog keista ir neįdomu.`
- `Rinkčiausi paprastesnį variantą.`
- `Šitas vizualiai įdomesnis.`
- `Sunku įvardyti.`

## Main confounds

- novelty / salience
- error-detection bias
- aesthetics
- CS uncertainty
- aversion to asymmetry or anomaly

## Falsification question

If the anomaly is chosen because it is merely more salient, more beautiful, or obviously incorrect, it does not provide defensible epistemic-AW evidence.

## Status

**PROMISING / HIGH NOVELTY-SALIENCE RISK**

# Comparative view

| Candidate | Primary attraction | Main threat | Current status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1 — mechanism** | understand how something works | collapses into CS clarity / technical familiarity | PROMISING / HIGH-RISK |
| **E2 — pattern** | search for a rule | puzzle ability / closure / CS | HIGH-RISK |
| **E3 — anomaly** | inspect an unexpected relation | novelty / salience / error detection | PROMISING / HIGH-RISK |

There is intentionally **no winner yet**.

---

## Next step before asset generation

Do not generate X/Y assets yet.

First perform a concept-level red-team on these three candidates with the following narrow questions:

1. Is the candidate still meaningfully about response orientation / attention investment, or has it become CS/CR?
2. Can both alternatives be legitimately attractive?
3. Is the scene interesting without becoming a puzzle, threat, story, or obvious preference test?
4. Can plausible reactions include both AW-relevant and honest `NONE` paths?
5. Is the dominant confound controllable at image-design level?

Only a surviving concept should proceed to a MASTER image.

---

## Explicit non-goals

This candidate pool does not:

- validate AW
- establish a latent construct
- generalize one person's examples to all participants
- set psychometric thresholds
- define Gate D mapping rules
- require immediate human testing
- require Block A / Block B assignment

Its sole purpose is to find a falsifiable, visually viable epistemic-AW stimulus family candidate.
