## N0-004 — Spatial / Environmental
**Provisional axis:** AW

---

### N0-004-C1 — Forest path: continues vs. ends at clearing

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A narrow forest path continuing ahead, disappearing into trees — direction is forward, destination unknown |
| Image B concept | The same path arriving at an open clearing — the path ends, open space begins |
| Provisional axis | AW |
| Methodological value | Contrast between continuation-into-unknown vs. arrival-at-openness. Neither state is obviously preferable — continuation can read as freedom or unease; arrival can read as relief or exposure. Supports multiple genuine reactions. |
| Alternative interpretations | Could activate CS (do I know what's ahead?) or CR (am I in control of where I'm going?). Axis is not locked. |
| Visual confounds | Forest density may introduce safety/threat valence independent of the path direction. Lighting between the two images must be matched — dappled forest light vs. open-sky clearing naturally differ in brightness. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Medium. Open clearing may read as more positive (arrival, resolution) for some participants; path-into-trees may read as more positive (freedom, exploration) for others. Risk is reasonably symmetric. |
| Cue-leading risk | Low if cues stay at reaction level ("I want to keep going" / "I feel like I've arrived"). High if cues name the axis ("I prefer the known destination"). |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No manufactured objects. No barrier or threshold concept. No container. Uses natural environment and implied movement rather than object state. |
| Preliminary decision | **ADVANCE** |
| Human decision needed | Lighting match between forest interior and open clearing is a production challenge — confirm whether this is achievable before selecting as final. |

### N0-004-C2 — Urban courtyard: enclosed vs. opening onto street

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | An enclosed courtyard — walls on all sides, a contained space, one doorway visible but not emphasized |
| Image B concept | The same or equivalent courtyard with its opening toward a street visible and prominent — space connects outward |
| Provisional axis | AW |
| Methodological value | Enclosed/connected contrast without using a gate or barrier as the primary element — the spatial relationship is the contrast, not an object's state. |
| Alternative interpretations | CR reading is plausible: enclosed = contained/controlled; open to street = released/exposed. CS reading weaker here. |
| Visual confounds | Urban context introduces social associations (city noise, people outside) that natural scenes avoid. If the street side shows any people or movement, it introduces social content — forbidden for this slot. Background must be empty. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Medium-high. "Enclosed courtyard" may carry protective/safe valence; "opening onto street" may carry exposed/unsafe valence for some participants — or the reverse (trapped vs. free). Cultural context affects this significantly. |
| Cue-leading risk | Medium. Cues about "feeling contained" could make CR axis visible. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | Spatial rather than object-based. No gate, barrier, or threshold as primary element. |
| Preliminary decision | **HOLD** |
| Human decision needed | Urban context introduces cultural valence that is harder to control than natural environments. Recommend advancing C1 first; revisit C2 only if C1 fails production. |

### N0-004-C3 — Staircase: ascending into light vs. descending into shadow

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A staircase viewed from below, ascending — light visible at the top |
| Image B concept | The same or equivalent staircase viewed from above, descending — shadow at the bottom |
| Provisional axis | AW |
| Methodological value | Directional contrast using architecture rather than natural environment. |
| Alternative interpretations | Plausible AW probe, but the light/shadow contrast is a strong confound. |
| Visual confounds | Light at top / shadow at bottom is one of the most universally loaded visual metaphors in many cultural contexts (ascent = positive, descent = negative). This risks pre-resolving the contrast before any cue. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | High. Light-at-top carries near-universal positive valence in many cultural traditions. This pair may not produce genuine interpretive ambiguity. |
| Cue-leading risk | High — the visual itself leads interpretation strongly. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | Architectural rather than object-based. |
| Preliminary decision | **REJECT** |
| Human decision needed | None — rejection is driven by structural valence asymmetry that cannot be corrected by cue wording. |

## N0-005 — Process / Temporal
**Provisional axis:** CS

---

### N0-005-C1 — Plant: early sprout vs. established growth

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A single small sprout in soil — early stage, growth direction unclear, outcome open |
| Image B concept | The same species as an established plant — form defined, growth direction clear, outcome visible |
| Provisional axis | CS |
| Methodological value | Process contrast where neither state is "finished" vs. "broken" — both are valid stages. Early sprout supports ambiguity-tolerance readings; established plant supports clarity-seeking readings. Avoids the implicit "finished = better" problem. |
| Alternative interpretations | CR reading plausible: established plant = something that has been tended/controlled; sprout = something unformed/open. AW weaker here. |
| Visual confounds | Pot size, soil quality, and background must be matched. Different plant species would introduce a confound — both images should show the same species at different growth stages. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Low-to-medium. Both stages carry positive associations in different registers (potential vs. achievement). Symmetry is reasonably good. |
| Cue-leading risk | Low if cues stay at reaction level. Risk if cues name "knowing" or "not knowing" explicitly. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No container, no barrier, no manufactured object style contrast. Uses biological process state as the contrast. |
| Preliminary decision | **ADVANCE** |
| Human decision needed | Same-species matching across growth stages may require generated images — confirm production path before finalizing. |

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A dining table partially set — some items placed, others missing, setup in progress |
| Image B concept | The same table fully and symmetrically laid — complete, nothing missing |
| Provisional axis | CS |
| Methodological value | Process state contrast in a domestic context. Partial setup supports ambiguity-tolerance (things are in motion, outcome unclear); complete setup supports clarity-seeking (everything in its place). |
| Alternative interpretations | CR reading plausible: complete table = order/control; incomplete = openness/flexibility. |
| Visual confounds | "Fully laid" table may carry anticipation/positive valence (a meal is coming) independent of clarity-seeking. "Partially laid" may carry disorder/anxiety valence. These associations could overpower the intended CS contrast. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Medium. Complete table is likely more universally read as positive (readiness, order) than partial setup. |
| Cue-leading risk | Medium — "everything in its place" phrasing in cues could make CR axis visible. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No container, no barrier, no object style contrast. Uses domestic process state. |
| Preliminary decision | **HOLD** |
| Human decision needed | Valence asymmetry risk needs human assessment — is "complete table = positive" strong enough to undermine genuine CS ambiguity? |

## N0-006 — Texture / Material
**Provisional axis:** CR

---

### N0-006-C1 — Surface: rough stone vs. smooth stone

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A close-up of rough, unfinished stone surface — irregular, varied, tactilely complex |
| Image B concept | The same type of stone, polished smooth — uniform, regular, tactilely simple |
| Provisional axis | CR |
| Methodological value | Material contrast without open/closed, style, or process framing. Rough surface can support reactions of unpredictability/resistance; smooth surface can support reactions of control/resolution. Neither is universally preferred. |
| Alternative interpretations | CS reading plausible: smooth = clarity, rough = ambiguity. AW weaker — approach/withdrawal toward a surface is less natural than CR or CS. |
| Visual confounds | Lighting must be matched — polished stone reflects light differently from rough stone by nature. Difference in reflectivity is a genuine confound that cannot be fully eliminated; must be minimized by shooting both at the same angle and light source. Color variation between rough and polished versions of the same stone is also a potential confound. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Low-to-medium. Neither texture carries a universal positive valence — preferences are genuinely individual. |
| Cue-leading risk | Low if cues stay at felt reaction. Risk if cues name "structure" or "order" explicitly. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No manufactured object, no barrier, no container, no process state. Pure material/surface contrast. |
| Preliminary decision | **ADVANCE** |
| Human decision needed | Lighting and reflectivity management — confirm production approach (photograph vs. generation) before finalizing. |

### N0-006-C2 — Surface: woven textile vs. plain textile

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A close-up of a woven or textured textile — pattern visible, structure complex |
| Image B concept | The same weight of fabric in a plain, uniform weave — no visible pattern, surface even |
| Provisional axis | CR |
| Methodological value | Textile contrast is tactilely intuitive and carries fewer cultural/architectural associations than stone. Woven pattern can support structured/predictable readings; plain surface can support open/minimal readings. |
| Alternative interpretations | CS reading plausible (pattern = readable, plain = ambiguous). |
| Visual confounds | Color must be identical between the two — a patterned textile in one color vs. a plain textile in another would introduce color valence. Same hue, different weave structure only. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Low. Both textile types are common and neither is culturally marked as superior in the same way as, e.g., "clean vs. dirty." |
| Cue-leading risk | Low. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No footwear (P0-001 uses shoes — textile contrast must not use clothing or worn items). Pure surface contrast. |
| Preliminary decision | **ADVANCE** |
| Human decision needed | Confirm that the textile context (color matching, no clothing association) is achievable in production. |

## N0-007 — Direction / Orientation
**Provisional axis:** AW

---

### N0-007-C1 — Object facing toward vs. away from viewer

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A neutral object (e.g., a chair, a small boat, a simple vessel) oriented toward the camera — front faces viewer |
| Image B concept | The same object in the same setting, oriented away from the camera — back faces viewer |
| Provisional axis | AW |
| Methodological value | Directional contrast using orientation rather than object state. "Facing toward" and "facing away" are both interpretively open — toward can mean invitation or confrontation; away can mean departure or privacy. |
| Alternative interpretations | CR reading possible (object facing me = something I must respond to; away = released from that). CS weaker. |
| Visual confounds | The object chosen must not carry strong associations by itself (e.g., an animal facing toward/away would introduce biological valence). A simple geometric or domestic object minimizes this. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Low-to-medium. "Toward" may carry slightly more engagement/positive valence, but this is less universal than light/dark or open/closed. |
| Cue-leading risk | Low if cues name the felt relation to the object's orientation, not the orientation itself. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No barrier or threshold (P0-002). No style contrast (P0-001). No container (P0-003). Uses orientation as the sole variable. |
| Preliminary decision | **ADVANCE** |
| Human decision needed | Object selection — what specific neutral object minimizes intrinsic associations? Requires human judgment before production. |

### N0-007-C2 — Path or road: toward viewer vs. away from viewer

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A path photographed so it leads toward the camera — receding point is behind the viewer's position |
| Image B concept | The same path photographed so it leads away from the camera — receding point is ahead |
| Provisional axis | AW |
| Methodological value | Strong directional contrast using a minimal, familiar visual element. |
| Alternative interpretations | Plausible AW probe. CS and CR weaker for a simple path. |
| Visual confounds | Perspective itself changes significantly between the two shots (foreground/background relationship reverses), which may make the images feel more different than intended even with matched lighting and location. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Medium. "Path leading away" carries departure/journey associations; "path toward viewer" carries arrival/return associations. Both can be read positively or negatively, but the associations are strong. |
| Cue-leading risk | Medium — path direction strongly implies movement direction, which is close to the AW axis concept. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No object, no barrier, no container. Pure directional/perspective contrast. |
| Preliminary decision | **HOLD** |
| Human decision needed | The perspective reversal between shots is a structural visual confound — assess whether it can be controlled enough to be methodologically usable. |

## N0-008 — Light / Weather
**Provisional axis:** CS or CR — [PROVISIONAL]

---

### N0-008-C1 — Same outdoor scene: overcast vs. diffuse sunlight

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | An outdoor scene (courtyard, field edge, or simple exterior) under flat, overcast light — no shadows, even illumination, sky grey-white |
| Image B concept | The same scene under diffuse sunlight — soft shadows present, sky warm-white or pale blue, no harsh contrast |
| Provisional axis | CS or CR [PROVISIONAL] — overcast may probe ambiguity-tolerance (CS) or openness/release (CR); sunlit may probe clarity-seeking (CS) or structure/predictability (CR). Cannot confirm without reviewing interpretive field in context. |
| Methodological value | Lighting as the sole variable, with both states being mild — avoiding the high-valence drama of "stormy vs. sunny." Diffuse sunlight rather than direct sunlight reduces the "sunny = obviously better" asymmetry. |
| Alternative interpretations | AW reading possible (overcast = withdrawal, sunlit = approach), but weaker than CS or CR. |
| Visual confounds | Time of day must be matched — overcast and sunlit versions of the same scene at different times of day introduce shadow angle and color temperature changes beyond the intended contrast. Both should be shot at the same time of day with weather as the only variable. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Low-to-medium. Diffuse sunlight is more positive than overcast in most cultural contexts, but less so than direct bright sun. The contrast is mild enough that genuine ambiguity may be preserved. |
| Cue-leading risk | Low if cues name felt reaction to the light quality, not the weather condition itself. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | No object, no barrier, no container, no process state. Ambient condition as sole variable — fully novel in library. |
| Preliminary decision | **ADVANCE** |
| Human decision needed | Axis confirmation requires seeing the specific concept with candidate cues — cannot be determined from the concept description alone. |

### N0-008-C2 — Same indoor scene: window light vs. artificial light

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A simple interior space lit only by natural window light — direction of light clear, shadows soft, quality variable |
| Image B concept | The same space lit by even artificial light — no directional shadow, uniform illumination |
| Provisional axis | CS or CR [PROVISIONAL] |
| Methodological value | Light source contrast (natural/directional vs. artificial/even) without changing the scene's content. |
| Alternative interpretations | Natural light may carry warmth/organic valence; artificial light may carry clinical/controlled valence — these associations could overpower the intended axis contrast. |
| Visual confounds | Natural window light changes with time of day and season, making it difficult to produce a truly matched pair unless generated. The quality difference between natural and artificial light is also a strong aesthetic signal that may introduce design/taste preferences unrelated to AW/CS/CR. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Medium-high. Natural light is frequently associated with warmth and preference in many design and lifestyle contexts. |
| Cue-leading risk | Medium — "natural vs. artificial" carries connotations that could lead cue selection independent of axis. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | Novel light source contrast. |
| Preliminary decision | **HOLD** |
| Human decision needed | The natural-vs-artificial light aesthetic association may be too strong to control. Assess whether C1 (outdoor overcast vs. diffuse sun) suffices before pursuing C2. |

### N0-009-C1 — Two figures: distant vs. close, backs to camera

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | Two human figures seen from behind, standing far apart in a neutral outdoor setting — spatial distance is large, no interaction implied |
| Image B concept | The same two figures in the same setting, standing close together — spatial distance is small, no interaction implied |
| Provisional axis | [UNRESOLVED] — proximity can activate AW (approach/withdrawal between the viewer and the scene), CS (clarity or ambiguity of the relationship), or CR (controlled vs. open spatial boundary). |
| Methodological value | Figures seen from behind remove face and expression as variables. Spatial distance is the sole contrast. No narrative is implied by posture — figures simply stand. |
| Alternative interpretations | All three axes are plausible. Axis must be determined by candidate cue review, not by the concept description. |
| Visual confounds | Even without faces, figure silhouette, clothing style, and relative height can imply gender, age, or relationship. Figures should be as visually neutral as possible — similar silhouettes, no distinctive clothing. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Medium. "Close together" may read as warmth/connection; "far apart" may read as isolation/solitude. Both can be read positively or negatively — this is the core methodological bet for this pair. |
| Cue-leading risk | High risk if cues attribute emotion or relationship to the figures. Cues must address the participant's reaction to the spatial arrangement, not the figures' assumed state. |
| Projection check | Backs-to-camera reduces projection risk significantly compared to faces or profiles. Figures must not be posed in ways that imply a specific relationship (argument, reunion, waiting for someone). |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | Fully novel — first pair in library with implied social presence. |
| Preliminary decision | **ADVANCE** (conditional on passing projection and neutrality audit before production) |
| Human decision needed | Silhouette neutrality and clothing guidance require human decision before production brief is written. |

### N0-009-C2 — Occupied vs. empty bench

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Image A concept | A park or outdoor bench with one person seated — presence implied, space shared |
| Image B concept | The same bench, empty — no presence, space unoccupied |
| Provisional axis | [UNRESOLVED] |
| Methodological value | Social presence implied by a single figure rather than two — removes the relationship-between-figures confound. The contrast is presence/absence rather than proximity. |
| Alternative interpretations | AW reading strong (would I approach or avoid a bench with someone on it?). CS reading possible (clarity vs. ambiguity of the social situation). |
| Visual confounds | A seated figure, even from a distance, implies a person with a state, intention, or story. Projection risk is higher than C1 — the single figure is more narratively loaded than two neutral standing figures. |
| Valence asymmetry risk | Medium. "Empty bench" can read as peaceful or lonely; "occupied bench" can read as welcoming or intrusive. Asymmetry direction is participant-dependent, which is the intended effect — but the single figure may carry more uncontrolled projection than the concept can handle. |
| Cue-leading risk | High if cues name the person's state. Must be kept strictly at the level of the participant's felt reaction to the spatial arrangement. |
| Projection check | Single seated figure is a higher projection risk than backs-to-camera pair. Participant is likely to attribute mood, intention, or social role to the figure. |
| Differentiation from P0-001–P0-003 | Novel social content. |
| Preliminary decision | **HOLD** |
| Human decision needed | Projection risk assessment — is a single seated figure too narratively loaded for the neutrality standard? Recommend advancing C1 first; C2 only if C1 fails the calibration audit. |

## Open questions for human methodological review

1. **N0-004-C1 production** — can lighting between forest interior and open clearing be matched adequately in photography, or does this require generated images?

2. **N0-005-C2 valence** — is "complete table = positive" strong enough to undermine genuine CS ambiguity, or is the domestic context neutral enough to preserve it?

3. **N0-006-C1 reflectivity** — is the lighting/reflectivity difference between rough and polished stone a tolerable known limitation, or a disqualifying confound?

4. **N0-007-C1 object selection** — which specific neutral object minimizes intrinsic directional associations? (Chair, vessel, simple geometric form, other?)

5. **N0-008 axis** — axis for the light/weather pair cannot be confirmed without candidate cues. Who reviews this, and at what stage?

6. **N0-009-C1 silhouette neutrality** — what specific guidance should the production brief give about figure silhouettes and clothing to minimize uncontrolled projection?

7. **N0-009 conditional acceptance gate** — who makes the final decision on whether C1 passes the projection and neutrality audit before production begins?
