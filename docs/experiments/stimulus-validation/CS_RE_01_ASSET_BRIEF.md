## Experimental question

Can two visually equivalent scenes differ in how clearly the relationship between two elements can be inferred, without turning the task into structure preference, puzzle solving, story completion, or simple object visibility?

The participant is never told that a relationship exists or what it means.

## MASTER scene

A calm, visually engaging but non-decorative studio / gallery tabletop scene viewed from a fixed slightly elevated front angle.

Two neutral sculptural ceramic modules sit on the same long stone plinth, one on the left and one on the right. The modules are clearly separate objects and neither is dominant.

A single thin matte neutral connector (cord / flexible tube / narrow linkage) runs between the two modules across the rear half of the plinth. The connector is visually secondary: visible on inspection, but not the first high-salience object in the scene.

A narrow vertical neutral screen stands between the modules but slightly behind them. Its position is chosen so it can later hide only a central section of the connector while leaving both ceramic modules completely visible.

No people, text, symbols, arrows, labels, brands, damage, threat, navigation cues, or obvious task affordance.

## Intended manipulation

Only the **amount of directly visible evidence that the connector links the two modules** changes.

### More-evidence variant

The connector path between the two modules is visually continuous enough that the relation can be inferred quickly.

### Less-evidence variant

A central portion of the same connector is hidden behind the neutral screen. Both endpoints remain plausible and both modules remain fully visible, but the connection is less immediately certain.

The screen must not hide either ceramic module.

## EXPERIENCE requirement

Both variants must remain legitimate choices.

Plausible spontaneous reasons include:

- "Aiškiau matau, kaip jie susiję."
- "Man patinka, kai ryšys matomas."
- "Įdomiau, kai ne iš karto aišku, ar jie susiję."
- "Nenoriu, kad viskas būtų iškart paaiškinta."
- "Tiesiog gražesnė kompozicija." → possible NONE
- "Sunku pasakyti." → insufficient

## INTERPRETABILITY / post-hoc paths

Possible intended-domain evidence:

- clarity of relation
- certainty / uncertainty about connection
- preference for directly available vs partially inferential relationship information

Possible cross-load / confounds:

- CR: connector interpreted mainly as structural organization
- partial-reveal family contamination
- aesthetics / symmetry
- utility / function speculation
- curiosity / novelty
- puzzle-solving framing

## Failure conditions

REJECT or REVISE if any of the following dominates:

1. Participant sees a puzzle with a correct answer.
2. One variant looks broken, incomplete, or defective.
3. The screen hides one of the two main modules.
4. The connector becomes the visual focal point.
5. Choice language is mainly about neatness / organization rather than relation clarity.
6. The two variants differ materially in composition or aesthetics beyond the intended relation evidence.
7. The scene invites a strong functional story (e.g. machine input/output) that overwhelms the information-clarity question.
